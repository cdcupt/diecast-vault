★ At a glance

Turn a shelf of physical Mini GT diecast cars into a 3D collection you carry in your pocket — scan a real car, get a rotatable model bonded to its catalog number, and (optionally) share that scan so other owners never have to scan the same car twice.

1 Problem & insight

Erik owns a sizeable physical Mini GT (1:64) diecast collection, displayed in a wall cabinet. Three frictions motivate going digital:

Not portable

To show a model to a friend you must physically carry a fragile, cased car. You can’t share the shelf when you’re away from it.

No digital twin exists

Mini GT publishes no official 3D models, and no open library exists. The only way to get a digital twin of your exact car is to capture it yourself.

Effort doesn’t compound

If every collector scans the same popular release in isolation, the work is wasted. A shared, catalog-keyed library means each release is scanned once for everyone.

Insight: the catalog number is the universal join key. Bond every scan to its Mini GT release number and the collection becomes both a personal 3D shelf and a community asset that gets richer with every contribution.

3 Goals & non-goals

Goals

  • Scan a physical diecast into a usable 3D model entirely on the iPhone.
  • Bond every model to its Mini GT catalog number + edition metadata.
  • Browse the collection as a fast 3D grid; rotate & view any car in AR.
  • Local-first storage with optional iCloud sync across the owner’s devices.
  • Opt-in community library, deduplicated by catalog number, so scans are reusable.
  • Feel finished: smooth capture guidance, graceful empty/error states.

Non-goals (v1)

  • 3D printing / mesh export (explicitly dropped by PM).
  • Android or web viewer (iOS-only; revisit later).
  • Marketplace, payments, trading, valuations.
  • A social feed / following / comments.
  • Brands beyond Mini GT (architecture stays brand-extensible, but content focus is Mini GT).

5 Scope (v1)

In · build now

Guided scan, on-device reconstruction, catalog bonding, 3D grid, rotate + AR viewer, local store, iCloud sync, opt-in community upload/browse/pick, lightweight identity for attribution.

Later · designed-for

Server-side reconstruction fallback for older devices, manual model touch-up, collection stats, web share link for non-iOS friends, brand expansion beyond Mini GT.

Out · not v1

3D printing / STL export, Android app, payments / marketplace, social feed, AI auto-recognition of the car (nice future, not now).

9 Storage feasibility ⏳ validating

This section answers your direct question — “can/should I store all the models on my VPS, given the storage requirements?” — with real numbers from the in-flight research pass.

⏳ Pending research (workflow in flight). The kickoff research pass is computing: typical USDZ size per scan at low/med/high texture; collection-size math (50 / 200 / 500 cars); the deduped shared-library ceiling (bounded by the number of distinct Mini GT SKUs, not by user count); object-storage choice (local FS vs MinIO vs R2/B2, weighing egress); and a monthly cost envelope. I’ll replace this box with the verdict + numbers the moment it lands.

Current working assumption (to be confirmed): a small-object USDZ runs ~5–40 MB. Because the shared library is deduplicated by catalog number, its size is capped by the count of distinct Mini GT releases (low thousands), not by how many users upload — so the entire canonical catalog is plausibly tens of GB, which a VPS with object storage handles comfortably. Egress (community downloads) is the variable to watch; thumbnails-first browsing keeps it down. Verdict: likely “yes, feasible” — final numbers pending.

10 IP & community-sharing posture ⏳ validating

Redistributing user scans of licensed cars from your server is the one area with real legal nuance, so it’s being researched rather than guessed.

⏳ Pending research (workflow in flight). Computing how comparable platforms (Sketchfab, Printables, Polycam) handle user-uploaded scans of branded objects + takedowns, and which mitigations are worth baking into v1 defaults.

Default-safe posture we’ll adopt unless research says otherwise:

  • Personal-use by default, sharing strictly opt-in and revocable.
  • No trademark in the product name (hence the “Diecast Vault” working title, not “Mini GT …”).
  • Uploader attestation + clear ToS; a DMCA-style takedown path and a model status flag in the schema (already in §7).
  • Dropping 3D printing reduces exposure — view-first, and whether the community lets you download the mesh vs only view it is a PM decision (see §13).

11 Success criteria

Product / quality bar

  • A first-time user can scan a car and see it rotating in the grid in under ~5 minutes, unaided.
  • Reconstruction succeeds on a “reasonable” scan of a representative Mini GT car on a target iPhone.
  • “Show a friend” (grid → spinning 3D / AR) is one tap.
  • Both beta-testers sign “ship” before Gate 3.

Engineering bar

  • Capture → reconstruction is fully on-device on supported hardware.
  • Community upload/pick round-trips against the VPS with auth + dedup by catalog no.
  • No data loss: local-first, with iCloud sync verified across two devices.
  • VPS deploy coexists with existing tenants (own DB/role/port/vhost).

13 Open questions for the PM

None of these block the Design pass — I’ll default safely and you can correct at Gate 1 — but they’re the genuine product calls:

Community models: view-only in-app, or also downloadable to the owner’s device? (Affects IP posture and storage egress.) Default: add-to-shelf for viewing, no raw mesh export.
Product name — keep “Diecast Vault”, or something else? (Staying off the “Mini GT” trademark is the only hard constraint.)
iCloud sync in v1, or v1.1? Default: build local-first now, ship iCloud as a fast-follow if it risks the v1 timeline.
Identity: is Sign in with Apple acceptable as the only login (needed for attribution + takedowns on community uploads)? Default: yes.
