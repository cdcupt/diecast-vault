★ At a glance

Turn a shelf of physical Mini GT diecast cars into a 3D collection you carry in your pocket. Anyone can browse the shared catalog, pick a model another owner already scanned, and spin it in 3D/AR to show a friend. Pro-iPhone owners scan their physical cars to fill that shared library — each release captured once, for everyone. Tap any model to meet the real car it replicates — its history, specs, and reference photos.

1 Problem & insight

Erik owns a sizeable physical Mini GT (1:64) diecast collection in a wall cabinet. Three frictions motivate going digital:

Not portable

To show a model you must carry a fragile, cased car. You can’t share the shelf when you’re away from it.

No digital twin exists

Mini GT publishes no official 3D models, and there is no open library or data API. A digital twin must be captured by someone who owns the car.

Effort doesn’t compound

If every collector scans the same release in isolation, the work is wasted. A catalog-keyed shared library means each release is scanned once for everyone.

Insight: capturing is scarce (only Pro iPhones can scan, and small glossy cars are hard), so the durable value isn’t the scanner — it’s the shared, deduplicated library keyed by catalog number + drive. Make scanning a contribution and picking the default, and the collection becomes a community asset that gets richer with every upload while most users never touch a scanner.

3 Goals & non-goals

Goals

  • View & pick on any iOS 17 iPhone — browse catalog, add a community scan, rotate & show in AR.
  • Scan on Pro iPhones — guided on-device capture → USDZ, gated at runtime on isSupported.
  • Bond every model to its catalog number + drive (+ edition stamp as a per-copy attribute).
  • Learn the real car — every detail screen carries the real vehicle’s history + specs and reference photos to compare against your model.
  • Local-first storage with optional iCloud sync of the owner’s collection.
  • Opt-in community library, deduped by number+drive, one canonical scan per release.
  • Feel finished: clear capability messaging, graceful empty/error/offline states.

Non-goals (v1)

  • Mesh / USDZ export and 3D printing (view-only — also lowers IP risk).
  • Android or web viewer (iOS-only; revisit later).
  • Server-side reconstruction for non-Pro devices (deferred fallback).
  • Marketplace, payments, valuations, social feed.
  • Brands beyond Mini GT (architecture stays brand-extensible; content focus is Mini GT).

Gate-1 go/no-go: a Spike-0 scan-quality trial on 3–5 representative Mini GT cars (using the Apple Guided Capture sample / USDZScanner as-is) must show output that’s “good enough to show a friend” before we commit to building the scan UI. 9 Storage feasibility ✓ answered

Your direct question — “can/should I store all these on my VPS, given storage requirements?” — answered with numbers.

Verdict: yes — host it, but split storage. Put metadata in Postgres on the shared VPS instance (own DB+role) and model blobs in Cloudflare R2. Do not serve USDZ files off the VPS disk/bandwidth — that would burn BWH’s 1–3 TB/mo cap and risk degrading 9relay/BillMind.

The sizing math

One reduced-detail 1:64 scan ≈ 1–4 MB (medium ≈ 3–8 MB). The deduped universe is bounded by the Mini GT catalog: ~1,500 SKUs ever, ×2 for LHD/RHD ≈ 3,000 slots. At ~5 MB that’s a ~15 GB fully-populated ceiling; realistic operating point 5–10 GB.

The cost envelope

Inside R2’s free tier (10 GB + 1M writes + 10M reads/mo). Even 50 GB ≈ $0.75/mo, and community downloads are $0 egress. Net: ~$0–2/month for years. No new VPS spend — only Postgres rows + a Caddy vhost.

Why R2, not MinIO or B2: MinIO on the budget box would route every download through VPS bandwidth (hurting co-tenants); B2 only gets free egress when fronted by Cloudflare anyway, so R2’s flat zero-egress is the simpler equivalent for a download-heavy feature. Personal (non-shared) collections never touch the VPS at all — ~200 cars ≈ 1 GB on-device + optional iCloud (USDZ as CKAsset in the user’s private DB, counts against their iCloud quota). Cap accepted uploads (~25 MB) and standardize the shared library on reduced/medium detail to keep it bounded.

10 IP & community-sharing posture ✓ answered

Not legal advice — a practical risk read. The risk is real but proportionate and very manageable for a hobbyist app; the protections are standard and cheap.

Launch-blocker for the community feature: register a DMCA §512(c) Designated Agent with the U.S. Copyright Office ($6, renew every 3 yrs), publish the contact in-app, ship a notice-and-takedown flow, and adopt + enforce a repeat-infringer policy. Missing the agent registration alone forfeits safe harbor. This is the single highest-leverage mitigation — exactly what Thingiverse/Sketchfab/Polycam rely on.

What the law says

  • Scans aren’t independently copyrightable (Meshwerks v. Toyota, 10th Cir. 2008) — so users don’t “own” their scans. But a scan still reproduces protected badges, livery art, and trade dress baked into the car.
  • Trademark/trade dress is the real exposure (Ferrari is historically aggressive); redistributing uploads makes the dev a direct actor, not a passive host.
  • Nominative fair use covers “Mini GT MGT00748” as a metadata reference — but not putting the mark in the app name/icon/marketing.

v1 default-safe posture

  • Local/private by default; sharing strictly opt-in per model, revocable.
  • View-only — no mesh/USDZ export, no print path (removes the most-litigated harm).
  • No car-maker / “Mini GT” mark in the product name or chrome; catalog number is descriptive metadata only.
  • ToS uploader attestation at the share step; non-commercial license; rapid-delist on any brand objection (hobby-platform norm).
  • Real-car content: Wikipedia/Commons require attribution (CC-BY / BY-SA) — shown per photo and on the history blurb; AI-fallback images are badged “illustration”, never passed off as real.

11 Success criteria

Product / quality bar

  • Spike-0 passes: a representative Mini GT car scans into a model that’s convincingly “good enough to show a friend” (Gate-1 criterion).
  • A first-time user on any iPhone can find a release and add a community model to their shelf in under ~2 minutes.
  • “Show a friend” (grid → spinning 3D / AR) is one tap.
  • Both beta-testers sign “ship” before Gate 3.

Engineering bar

  • Scan + reconstruction on-device on a Pro iPhone; correct runtime gating elsewhere (no crashes, clear messaging).
  • Community pick/upload round-trips against the VPS + R2 with auth, dedup by (number, drive), presigned URLs.
  • No data loss: local-first, iCloud sync verified across two devices.
  • VPS deploy coexists with existing tenants (own DB/role/port/vhost; Caddy restarted after host edit).

13 Open questions for the PM

None block the Design pass — I’ll default as noted and you can correct at Gate 1 — but these are the genuine product calls the research surfaced:

Shared-library detail level: medium (~5 MB, best fidelity) or reduced (~3 MB, faster/cheaper)? Default: reduced for the canonical, to keep downloads snappy on mobile.
Curation model: auto-publish the first scan per (number, drive), admin approval, or community voting? Default: report-and-replace (publish, then flag/curate) for v1 — lowest ops.
Brand scope: Mini GT only for v1, or design the namespace for other 1:64 brands (Tarmac, Inno64)? Default: Mini GT only; keep schema brand-extensible.
Cloudflare R2: provision under the same org as your other apps, or a dedicated account for billing isolation? Default: same org unless you want isolation.
iCloud sync on by default (consumes the user’s iCloud quota) or opt-in? Default: opt-in; local-first works without it.
Ever allow mesh export post-v1? Default: no — view-only is the safety story; revisit only with explicit sign-off.
